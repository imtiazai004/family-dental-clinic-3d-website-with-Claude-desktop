Replacing a missing tooth

  1. Step 1 of 4

    The implant

    A small titanium post is placed in the jawbone where the root used to be. Over a few months, the bone bonds to it.

  2. Step 2 of 4

    The abutment

    A connector is fitted on top of the implant. It rises through the gum to hold the new tooth.

  3. Step 3 of 4

    The crown

    A crown made for your mouth, shaped and coloured to match your other teeth, is fixed onto the abutment.

  4. Step 4 of 4

    A tooth that works like your own

    You can bite, chew and smile normally again. With good care, an implant can last for many years.

Saving an infected tooth

  1. Step 1 of 4

    Infection reaches the pulp

    Deep decay or a crack lets bacteria into the pulp. This is what causes the throbbing pain.

  2. Step 2 of 4

    The canals are cleaned

    With the tooth numbed, the dentist removes the infected pulp through a small opening and cleans each canal with fine files.

  3. Step 3 of 4

    Filled and sealed

    The clean canals are filled with a rubber-like material and sealed so bacteria cannot get back in.

  4. Step 4 of 4

    Your own tooth, kept

    The tooth stays in place and works normally. Your dentist may recommend a crown to protect it.

Crowns and veneers

  1. Crowns

    A crown fits over a tooth that is cracked, heavily filled or weakened, and gives it back its full shape and strength.

  2. Veneers

    A veneer is a thin shell bonded to the front of a tooth. It covers chips, small gaps and stains that whitening cannot lift.

Smile treatments

  1. Braces

    Brackets and a thin wire apply gentle, steady pressure, moving crowded or crooked teeth into line over several months.

  2. Whitening

    A supervised treatment that lifts years of tea, coffee and smoking stains.
